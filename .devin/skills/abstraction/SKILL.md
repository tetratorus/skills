# Abstraction

Keep only the detail the task needs, freeing cognitive capacity for the reasoning that matters.

## Intuition

A representation costs resources to hold and manipulate. Every distinction we track consumes some of our limited attention, working memory, or computational capacity.

Abstraction changes that cost. By representing less, we gain capacity to do more: compare alternatives, follow longer chains of consequences, consider more interacting parts, or recognize relationships.

The value is not simply a smaller description. It is the thinking that the smaller representation makes possible.

## How to approach a problem

Start with what you need to do with the representation. Predict motion? Compare options? Explain behavior? Design an interface? The task determines which distinctions deserve space.

For each detail, ask:
- Does tracking this change a conclusion or action relevant to the task?
- What does it cost to retain and reason about?
- What additional thinking becomes possible if I leave it out?

Choose a representation that makes the needed operations cheap. A useful abstraction may replace many particulars with a relationship, a quantity, a category, or a contract.

Test it by doing the intended reasoning. Check whether omitted distinctions would materially change the result. Restore detail where it earns its cost; otherwise, leave it outside the working representation.

Change abstractions when the task changes. Details do not have fixed importance independent of what we are trying to do.

## Example

For reasoning about Earth's orbit, represent Earth as a point mass. Its terrain and internal structure need not occupy the working model. The capacity saved can go toward reasoning about gravitational interactions and trajectories.

For planning a landing, surface geometry becomes relevant. Use a representation that includes it.

Neither task requires carrying everything known about Earth. Each requires a representation suited to the operations we need to perform.

## Standard

Judge an abstraction by the useful reasoning it enables within the available cognitive or computational budget, while preserving the distinctions needed for the task.

A short label that still requires unpacking every detail has saved little. A useful abstraction lets us reason at its level.
