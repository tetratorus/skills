# PinkElephants

A pink elephant is content that survives because of how a thing evolved, not because of what it is for now. Remove it, and whatever exists only to support it. Denying it or announcing its removal keeps it alive. Anything that serves the present purpose stays, whatever its origin.

## Scope

Use the content or task currently under discussion. Ask one focused question if the target is unclear. Stay inside the user's stated boundaries and report what you found outside them. Match the request: a review locates and proposes, an edit changes.

## Application

1. **State the present purpose** and who holds it. Judge everything against that, never against its origin. Where the manner is the message—a quotation, a specimen, a parody, a voice the reader comes for—the style is the content. If cleaning it would destroy that purpose, say so and ask before touching it.

2. **Ask it of the whole before the parts.** Judge the artifact before polishing its contents. If it no longer serves a present purpose, remove it and relocate the live facts it uniquely holds. A completed plan, past-work record, or status snapshot may still serve as evidence, accountability, or an explanation of decisions. Keep it for that use, not merely because it exists.

3. **Separate residue from substance.** Look for process narration, completion markers, score-keeping, novelty and legacy labels, decoration, and restatement. These are candidates, not verdicts: remove them only when they serve no present purpose. Being recorded in version control does not by itself make information redundant where the reader needs it. Preserve requirements, constraints, evidence, unresolved findings, disclosed gaps, and the author's uncertainty. A deprecated path still needed during a migration is live. Where the artifact contradicts itself, correct it if the evidence settles it; otherwise state the uncertainty rather than inventing a resolution.

4. **Take the traces with it.** References, links, summaries, table-of-contents entries, names, and filenames that exist only to point at the removed concept go too. A pointer the reader still needs—to a source, a canonical location, the thing itself—is not one of them.

5. **Leave no mark of the edit.** Do not add cleanup notes, changelog entries, or comments merely to announce the removal. Preserve independently required change records and audit evidence. Reread the result whole: it should stand on its own, with needed meaning intact and no broken references or solely dependent traces.

## Output

A review reports each finding: where, what, why it lacks present justification, and the exact change.

An edit returns the changed artifact. Keep the accompanying summary short, and outside the artifact.
